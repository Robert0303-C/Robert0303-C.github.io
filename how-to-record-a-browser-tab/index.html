How to record a browser tab with audio (and your microphone) on Windows

Written by the developer of Chunked Screen Recorder (a solo project; code and issue history at github.com/Robert0303-C). Last updated 2026-10-16.

Short answer: you do not need to record your whole screen to capture one browser tab. Install a tab-recording extension, open the tab you want, click the extension icon, press Start — the extension captures that one tab's picture and its audio. Turn the microphone switch on if you also want your own voice on the file. Recording a single tab takes about 20 seconds to set up and produces a normal MP4 you can play immediately.

Key takeaways

  • Only one tab is recorded — other tabs, your desktop and your notifications are never in the file.
  • The tab's own audio is captured with the tab; your microphone is a separate, opt-in track mixed on your machine.
  • Tag or window capture is what "record a tab" means technically: the browser's chrome.tabCapture API hands the tab's video and audio streams to the recorder.
  • The recording is written to disk every 500 ms while it runs, as a playable MP4 — that interval is the maximum you can lose if the tab dies mid-recording.
  • Protected (DRM) video — Netflix, Disney+, some sports streams — comes out black. That is a platform restriction, not a setting you can change.

What counts as "recording a tab"?

A browser tab is not a screen region or a window. It is a rendering context that the browser can hand out separately, which is why a tab recording can include the tab's audio without picking up the podcast playing in your other tab, the meeting notification, or your desktop. The W3C Screen Capture specification makes that distinction explicit: it defines three capture surfaces — monitor, window and browser, the last being "a browser display surface, or single browser window".

Chromium browsers expose this through the tabCapture extension API, documented at developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/tabCapture. An extension asks for that stream, encodes it locally, and writes the result to a file. The encoder receives periodic data chunks; whether those chunks reach your disk during the recording or only at the end is the difference between losing nothing and losing everything (see the MediaRecorder API reference on MDN for how the recorder hands out data).

How to record a tab, step by step

  1. Install the recorder. In Microsoft Edge, open Chunked Screen Recorder in the add-ons store and click Get. Nothing else needs installing — no account, no companion app.
  2. Open the tab you want to record and leave it in front. Tab capture always records the tab that was active when you clicked the extension's icon.
  3. Click the extension icon in the toolbar. The popup shows which tab it will record.
  4. Want your voice on the recording? Switch the microphone on. The first time, Edge opens a small page that asks for microphone permission once — the permission prompt cannot be answered from the extension's own hidden window, so a visible page is used deliberately. After that the switch remembers your choice.
  5. Press Start recording. A desktop notification confirms it started; the recording continues even if you close the popup.
  6. Do what you were going to do — watch the lecture, run the demo, take the meeting. The file is already being written while this happens.
  7. Press Stop and pick a location in the "Save as" dialog. The file is a standard MP4 (H.264 video, AAC audio) that opens in any player.
  8. If it went wrong anyway, recover instead of re-recording. Open the extension again: unfinished recordings are listed, and the recovery page makes your own video player decode the file before it is called recovered.

Why recordings get lost — and what to do about it

Almost every "my recording is gone" story has the same shape: the recorder held the whole recording in memory and only wrote the file when you pressed stop. If the tab crashes, the browser is closed, or the machine sleeps and drops the session, the data was never on disk.

Chunked Screen Recorder inverts that: every 500 ms a finished MP4 fragment is closed and written to your disk, so the file on disk is a playable video for the entire recording. If a two-hour recording dies at 1:59:00, you still have 1:59:00 — the maximum loss is the last half second, not the whole session. Unfinished recordings also stay listed inside the extension, and the recovery page makes your own video player decode the file before it is called recovered, so "recovered" means "plays", not "a file exists".

Honest limits: the 500 ms window bounds what a crash can cost, it does not make it zero. This does nothing for a full disk, and it cannot help a recording you never started. If the source tab is protected content, the fragments are black from the first millisecond.

Frequently asked questions

Can I record just one tab's audio, without video?

You can mute your microphone and keep the tab's audio, but the output is still a video file containing video plus audio — the extension does not write audio-only files.

Why is my recording black?

Because protected video is built to be uncapturable. Windows implements that with a content-protection flag on SetWindowDisplayAffinity which, as Microsoft's own developer blog describes it, works by "returning black in screen captures of these windows" (New Ways to do Screen Capture, Windows Developer Blog, 16 September 2019). Netflix, Disney+ and similar players use exactly that mechanism — no extension can record them, and that is the point of it. The other common cause is unrelated: hardware-accelerated video on some GPUs. Turning off hardware acceleration in edge://settings/system and re-recording usually fixes that case.

Why does my recording have no sound?

In order: (1) the tab itself may have been silent; (2) if you wanted your own voice, the microphone switch must be on and its one-time authorisation granted — an unauthorised microphone yields a video with a silent audio track (measured at −91 dB above); (3) audio from other tabs is never captured, by design.

Can I record a Google Meet, Zoom or Teams call from the browser?

Yes — the tab's video and audio are captured like any other tab, and your microphone can be mixed in on top. Two caveats: recording other people may require their consent where you live, and a call running in a heavy web app produces a large file (about 1.2 MB per second at 1080p in our measurements).

Does it record my whole screen?

No. It records exactly one tab. Your desktop, other tabs, notifications and other windows are never in the file — which is also why a tab recording stays small and legible.

Where does the file go while it is still recording?

Into your own disk, in the browser's private extension storage, as playable MP4 fragments. When you press stop, the completed file is saved wherever you choose. Nothing is uploaded at any point.

Is there a Chrome version?

It is built on standard Chromium extension APIs, but as of 2026-10-16 it is published for Microsoft Edge only. The Edge listing is here.

What can't it do?

It does not record your desktop or other applications, it does not edit video, it does not upload or sync anything, and it does not keep a copy of your recording for you. It records a tab and writes the result to your disk; everything outside that is out of scope on purpose.

中文版：怎么录浏览器标签页的视频

同样的问题，中文说法是"怎么单独录一个浏览器标签页的视频（带声音、带自己的解说）"。 下面是要点；英文部分有完整的对比表和实测数据。最后更新 2026-10-16。

怎么录？

装一个标签页录制扩展 → 打开要录的那个标签页 → 点扩展图标 → 按"开始录制"。录的是 那一个标签页，你桌面上的其他东西、其他标签页、系统通知都不会进到文件里。想把自己的解说也录进去， 打开麦克风开关（第一次会让你在一个页面上授权一次）。

为什么录到一半丢了？

大多数录屏工具把整段录制先放在内存里，等你按"停止"才写文件——这中间崩了、关了浏览器，数据就没了。 这个扩展每 500 毫秒就把一个完整的 MP4 片段写到你的硬盘上，所以硬盘上的文件在录制期间一直是 能播放的：录了两小时崩在 1:59:00，你手上还有 1:59:00，最多丢最后半秒。

为什么录出来是黑的？

受保护的视频（Netflix、Disney+ 这类）任何录屏 API 都录不了，只能录成黑屏——这是平台限制，不是设置问题。 另一种情况是某些显卡的硬件加速，把 edge://settings/system 里的硬件加速关掉再录通常就好了。

为什么没有声音？

可能是那个标签页本身就没出声；也可能是你开了麦克风开关但还没授权——没授权的麦克风会录出一个 "画面正常、音轨静音（实测 −91 dB）"的文件。另外：别的标签页的声音是**故意**不录的。

会上传吗？收费吗？

不上传，也免费。扩展一共申请 6 个权限、没有任何网站访问权限，全程 零网络请求（包里唯一一处 fetch 读的是扩展自带的翻译文件）。细节见 隐私政策。
